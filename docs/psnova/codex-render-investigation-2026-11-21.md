## Four native-resolution window presents

The second F12 file, frame_117190.vfc, contains three presents of the Kuklonahda notification. The first matches the player's last screenshot most closely. Resizing the native replay to the cropped client area gave a mean absolute RGB error of 4.70 on the 0–255 scale, compared with 5.81 for the 4x replay. Lighting, the live compositor and sampling prevent exact pixel equality. [Measurement script](../../dump/codex-render-20261121/present-test/manual.py)

In the windowed A/B test with the old executable, the 4x game used the GPU image at frame 300, displayed the native guest buffer on frames 301–304, and returned to the GPU at 305. F12 synchronizes GPU targets to guest RAM. The window presenter used the GPU dirty flag as its only test of whether the detailed GPU image could be shown. A successful readback cleared that flag, including for a buffered frame about to be presented. No software draw or shader skip was involved.

Codex changed runtime/gfx/gfx_gpu.c. A scaled display target that has already been presented retains a native-size copy of the bytes transferred from the GPU. When the target is clean, the presenter compares the live guest bytes with the readback from the same GPU generation before choosing its full-resolution texture. A changed CPU image, a newer guest write, an explicit screenshot requiring CPU pixels or an unavailable snapshot still follows the guest path. The snapshot is released with the target. Offscreen targets and ordinary GPU-only frames do not copy pixels for this check. [Windowed test script](../../dump/codex-render-20261121/present-test/run.py)

Both synchronous capture downloads and asynchronous GPU readbacks use the same snapshot check. In a separate control, forcing asynchronous readbacks with no F12 capture caused all twelve recorded window presents to come from guest memory on the old build. The staged fix showed all twelve from the GPU under each test. A matching CPU buffer is checked byte-for-byte against GPU readback data rather than inferred from a dirty flag. The old playable executable is in present-test/game_host-before.exe for rollback.

The normal game_host.exe was rebuilt and tested again after deployment (SHA-256 CD8ED97B7F7C1D99B4DF8929F6F5D168F6CBD1C02C64F2022D2A9AB41DBAF2D3). Its 4x twelve-frame history showed 12 GPU and zero guest presentations both with F12 and with forced asynchronous readbacks outside capture. The normal depth-clip defaults are embedded. ## Missing party in the ruins

The player's fifth picture and the GPU replay of 116550 both show empty ruins. Removing depth clipping gives identical replay pixels. To isolate party geometry, Codex identified the vertex programs with bone indices and weights. The first world color pass submitted nine such draws (37–42 and 279–281). The same nine index addresses, vertex addresses, counts and strides appear in the following gameplay capture. Replaying those draws alone in the later camera shows Lutina. In the empty shot they produce zero visible actor pixels even with all scenery removed. The interpreter reports every triangle behind the eye for eight draws. The ninth also covers no pixel. [Party isolation comparison](../../dump/codex-render-20261121/characters/party-comparison.jpg)

As a control, Codex translated only the captured camera for these nine draws in a scratch copy. Lutina appears in her running pose with her original vertices, bone matrices and textures. In the original capture her body origin is about 35.5 game units behind the eye. In the gameplay shot the matched body origin is about 605 units in front of its camera. This establishes how she disappears in the recorded frame. The F12 files omit the 637 intervening frame numbers between their two three-present sequences. They do not establish where the camera should have been, when the party was supposed to appear or where the game placed other party members during that gap. No game camera or party placement override was installed. The Part 2 reference boss intro around 11:32 has a different composition and is insufficient as an exact camera reference for this shot. [Lutina recovered from the original capture](../../dump/codex-render-20261121/characters/ruins-reframed.png) · [Diagnostic script](../../dump/codex-render-20261121/characters/reframe.py)

The captured body transform and sampled skin rows already matched at the first memory snapshot and at draw 39. The same body origin stayed behind the eye in each of the three captured presents. In 117190, isolating the other bone-weighted submissions recovers the green player model. Those player's typical skinned submissions are absent from the first world pass in 116550. Lutina's relative position is therefore a different observation from the player's absent submissions. ## Recorded stalls

The live log records 21.51, 19.04 and 19.19 seconds for the three frames of the first capture; the second took 13.44, 10.52 and 10.73 seconds. Each made roughly one GPU target download per draw. Between recordings, frame 116562 took 449 ms. The live log then advanced to at least 124200. F12 recording takes a full memory baseline, synchronizes GPU targets and scans guest memory for changed pages at every draw. The presenter fix addresses image quality; F12 still has recording overhead. The two F12 files span 640 frames between their starts. A previous-frames history (F5) immediately after a future occurrence would cover more of the transition without repeating F12.
